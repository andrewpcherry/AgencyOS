Welcome to RequityAI
This guide explains how your role fits into AgencyOS — the operating system we use to run client delivery for our Facebook advertising agency.
AgencyOS is a connected stack: SmartSuite, Slack, Google Drive, Meta Business Manager, Meta Ads Manager, automation workflows, and managed intelligence agents. These tools work together so client onboarding, campaign setup, task assignment, QA, launch, and optimization all move in sync — without anything falling through the cracks.
Your role is to be the careful human operator inside this system. The system creates tasks, sends alerts, keeps records updated, and recommends next actions. You execute the work that still needs human eyes and human hands — and you do it with precision.


Contents

1. What RequityAI does
RequityAI is a Facebook advertising agency serving real estate investors and off-market property businesses. We help clients generate qualified seller leads using a structured Facebook and Instagram advertising system.
The campaign model has three stages. Understanding the stages helps you understand why specific operational tasks matter at specific moments.


The whole system moves a viewer from attention, to trust, to lead capture. AgencyOS monitors this flow and creates work for you when each stage needs a human action.

2. How AgencyOS keeps everything in sync
AgencyOS is the operational layer behind the agency. Instead of relying on memory, manual chasing, or scattered messages, it creates a clear, automatic sequence of work.
The end-to-end flow
A prospect books a call → the sales pipeline record is created or updated automatically.
Andrew runs the sales call.
When a deal is marked Won → the client record, onboarding record, Slack channels, and Drive folders are all created automatically.
The client moves through onboarding, with each step tracked in SmartSuite.
You complete the operational setup tasks the system assigns to you in Slack and SmartSuite.
When all prerequisites are met → the system creates the campaign build task.
You build the campaign in Meta Ads Manager, complete QA, and launch on approval.
Once live, campaign metrics are pulled in. The intelligence agent reviews performance and recommends a next action.
The system creates an optimization task → you execute, or escalate to Andrew if it requires judgment.


3. The tools you'll use
Slack
Slack is the daily communication layer. Task alerts, blocker reports, client updates, and operational summaries all flow through Slack. There are three types of Slack channels you need to understand.

SmartSuite
SmartSuite is the source of truth. It stores prospects, clients, onboarding state, campaigns, tasks, optimization reviews, and finance records — and it stores every SOP you'll need.


A few rules for SmartSuite hygiene:
Don't create records unless instructed.
Don't change fields unless a task or SOP tells you to.
Don't guess if a field is unclear — ask.
Google Drive
Google Drive is the content store. Client folders, raw videos, approved creative, onboarding documents, and script libraries all live in Drive. SmartSuite stores links to Drive files; the actual files stay in Drive.
A typical client folder contains a Raw Videos folder and an Ad Creative folder. The client uploads raw videos. The video editor processes them. Approved files are placed in the Ad Creative folder. Your job is to ensure the correct approved file is used when building campaigns.

Meta Business Manager
Meta Business Manager is where client business assets are managed: business portfolio access, ad account access, Facebook page access, Instagram account connection, and lead access. A major part of your role is confirming that RequityAI has the correct access before campaign work begins.
Meta access is one of the few areas where the system can't act for you — the client must approve access from inside their own Meta account. You may need to request access, check whether it's been granted, confirm the correct assets are visible, and report anything missing.
Meta Ads Manager
Meta Ads Manager is where campaigns are built, QA'd, and launched. This is the highest-accuracy area of your role.
A campaign should never be launched if you are unsure about: the ad account, campaign objective, Special Ad Category, targeting, budget, creative, lead form, or client identity. When in doubt, stop and ask Andrew.
Lead destinations
Different clients use different lead destinations: Meta Lead Centre, a CRM (such as GoHighLevel), a spreadsheet, or another integration. Your role is to confirm where leads should go and verify the destination is working before campaigns depend on it. If a test lead is required by the SOP, complete the test and confirm delivery.

4. How automations and agents help you
AgencyOS uses automation workflows and managed intelligence agents to remove manual coordination work. You don't need to know the technical names of the automations — you only need to know what they do, so you can work with them rather than against them.
What the automations do

What the managed agents do
Three managed intelligence agents support the system. They are not replacing the VA — they support the VA by watching data, creating clarity, and helping the system decide what should happen next.
Campaign Intelligence Agent
Reviews live campaign data — spend, leads, cost per lead, campaign age, status. Writes a recommended decision into SmartSuite. The recommendation may translate into an operational task for you (e.g., swap creative, build retargeting), or it may be flagged for Andrew if it requires strategic judgment.
Operations Supervisor Agent
Watches operational health: stuck records, slow movement, missed updates. Catches problems early so they don't accumulate.
Sync Agent
Translates structured Slack updates (when you use the approved /update workflow) into SmartSuite changes. Keeps SmartSuite current without forcing manual field-by-field updates.


5. Your operational responsibilities
Most coordination is automated. The work that remains is the work that needs human eyes and hands. Here's what you'll be doing day-to-day.
Onboarding setup
Phase 1 — Access setup
Confirm RequityAI has the correct Meta access for the client. Specifically:
Business portfolio access requested and approved by the client.
Correct ad account is visible.
Correct Facebook page is visible.
Correct Instagram account is connected.
Lead access is granted.
Client understands how lead notifications will work and has installed the Meta Ads app if required.
If access is missing, report exactly what is missing — not just "access is not working." Specificity speeds up the fix.
Phase 2 — Lead destination setup
Confirm where leads will go and verify the destination works.
Lead access available; Lead Centre visible if used.
Lead notifications turned on.
Client CRM or lead sheet connected if applicable.
Test lead delivered to the correct destination, if the SOP requires testing.
Phase 3 — Creative readiness
The client records videos using the provided scripts. Raw videos go into the Drive Raw Videos folder. The video editor produces approved ad creative and places it in the Ad Creative folder. Your job: ensure the approved file is what gets used at campaign build — never a raw file, never the wrong file.
Phase 4 — Campaign build
When access, leads, and creative are all ready, the system creates the campaign build task. You then build the campaign in Meta Ads Manager following the SOP.
Campaign build standards
Campaign build is the single most error-sensitive task in the agency. Before building, confirm: client, market, campaign type, budget, creative, and lead destination.
Inside Meta Ads Manager, verify every one of these settings before saving:

QA — the most important step
Campaign QA is not a formality. It's where mistakes are caught before money is spent. Open the campaign in Ads Manager and inspect the actual settings — don't rely on what the task says. If one item is wrong and the SOP allows you to fix it, fix it. If you're unsure, report it.

Launch & optimization
Once approval is given, publish the campaign and update the campaign status. Don't launch unless task, SOP, and approval are all clear.
After launch, the system will create optimization tasks based on the intelligence agent's review: keep running, pause, retarget, swap approved creative, adjust targeting, or flag for Andrew. Execute the operational ones; escalate the strategic ones. You're not expected to invent strategy — you're expected to execute approved changes accurately.

6. How to communicate
Slack updates
Slack is where work becomes visible. Updates should be short, clear, and specific — and they should always include enough information for someone else to act without asking you a follow-up question.


When reporting a blocker, include: client name, the tool, what you tried, what happened, what you checked, and what you need next.
Client channels vs. team channels
Client channels stay calm, professional, and simple. Don't over-explain internal processes. Don't mention technical errors. Don't blame tools, automation, Meta, or another team member. Don't discuss pricing, contracts, refunds, scope, strategy, or expected results — those are Andrew's conversations.
If a client asks something you can't answer, acknowledge politely and bring it to Andrew internally.

When to escalate to Andrew
Escalate when the task requires judgment, approval, or a decision outside the SOP. Specifically:
Client asks about pricing, contracts, refunds, scope, or expected results.
You're about to publish a campaign and something doesn't look right.
Campaign setup doesn't match the SOP.
Meta access is missing and you've already checked the obvious places.
Conflicting instructions across task, SOP, and tool.
The SOP is missing, unclear, or outdated.
A client seems upset or confused.
Anything risky.

Don't escalate things already documented in the SOP. Do escalate before guessing on something that could affect a client, campaign, budget, or data.

Open SmartSuite. Review your assigned tasks.

For each task

Open the linked SOP.

Open the correct tool (Meta Ads Manager, Drive, etc.).
Complete the assigned work, following the SOP step by step.
Check your work — especially in Meta Ads Manager, where small mistakes are expensive.
Update the task status.
Post a short confirmation in the team Slack channel if appropriate.
If blocked, report the blocker with full detail (client, tool, what you tried, what happened, what you need).
End of session
No completed tasks left without an update.
No blockers left unreported.
Nothing important sitting in your head that isn't visible to the system.

Campaigns built exactly to the SOP.
QA catches mistakes before launch — never after.
Slack updates are clear, specific, and don't require follow-up questions.

Blockers reported quickly with full context.
Approved creative used; raw files never used by accident.

AgencyOS works because every part has a role:


Your role is the human control point that keeps the system accurate. It is not a small role — without it, the automations have no eyes and no hands.

The most important thing to know on day one
All Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) live in SmartSuite. Every task you receive will either link directly to its SOP or point to the client / campaign record where the SOP can be found.
You are not expected to memorise every process. You are expected to open the task, read the SOP, follow it carefully, and update the system when the work is done.
If an SOP is missing, unclear, or contradicts what you see in the tool — stop and report it in the internal team channel. Never improvise around a missing SOP.
Stage | Campaign type | Goal
1 | Educational video | Build a warm audience using short, client-recorded videos. Identify viewers who are paying attention. The goal is attention, not an immediate lead.
2 | Retargeting | Reinforce the message to people who watched or engaged with stage one. Audiences are built later — once stage one has enough viewer data — not during onboarding.
3 | Lead capture | Capture seller leads via Meta lead forms, shown to the warmest audiences. The goal is a completed lead form delivered to the client or their CRM.
Why this matters for you
Your job is not to manage the agency from memory. The system holds the state. You follow the assigned task, complete the work, update the system, and report blockers.
If you complete work but don't update the task, the system will think it's still pending. If you update a task before the work is truly done, the system will move the client forward too early. Both create problems. Update only when the work is actually complete.
